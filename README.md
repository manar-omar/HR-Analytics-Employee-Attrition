# HR Analytics: Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview

Employee attrition is a critical challenge for organizations because losing employees can increase recruitment costs, reduce productivity, and negatively impact business performance.

This project analyzes employee attrition using Python and Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) techniques to identify the key factors associated with employee turnover and provide data-driven business recommendations.


## Business Problem

Why are employees leaving the company?

The objective of this project is to explore employee data, identify patterns related to attrition, and uncover insights that can help improve employee retention.


## Dataset

Dataset Source:

https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/pavansubhasht/ibm-hr-analytics-attrition-dataset

The dataset contains information about employee demographics, job roles, compensation, work conditions, satisfaction levels, and attrition status.

## Key Insights

1. Employees who work overtime have a significantly higher attrition rate compared to employees who do not work overtime.

2. Employees who left the company generally had lower monthly incomes than employees who remained.

3. Younger employees tend to leave the company more frequently than older employees.

4. Single employees exhibit the highest attrition rate compared to married and divorced employees.

5. Employees with low job satisfaction experience higher turnover rates.

6. Sales Representatives have the highest attrition rate among all job roles.

7. Employees who leave the company typically have fewer years at the company.


## Business Recommendations

- Reduce excessive overtime workloads.
- Review compensation strategies for lower-income employees.
- Develop retention programs targeting younger employees.
- Improve employee engagement and job satisfaction.
- Focus retention efforts on high-risk job roles.
- Strengthen onboarding programs for newly hired employees.
- Conduct regular employee satisfaction assessments.

## Conclusion

The analysis identified several factors associated with employee attrition, including overtime work, job satisfaction, monthly income, age, marital status, job role, and years at the company.

Employees working overtime, employees with lower salaries, younger employees, and employees in certain job roles showed higher attrition rates.

These findings can help organizations develop targeted retention strategies and improve workforce stability.
